# Competitor analysis: "Extreme" dot-to-dot books for adults (Amazon)

Source: the Amazon "Look inside" sample screenshots for two paperback listings,
captured 2026-10-03. Everything below comes from those screenshots, so the
claims about pages outside the sample are inferences and are marked as such.

---

## Book A: *100 Pages Hard Extreme Dot To Dot Puzzle Book For Adults*

| | |
|---|---|
| Author | P. G. N. Percy Grant |
| Format / price | Paperback, **$9.99** |
| Rating | 5.0 stars from **1 rating** (no real social proof yet) |
| Positioning | "Challenging connect-the-dots activities to boost focus, memory and relaxation"; the description says it is "specially designed for **seniors**" |
| Cover | Bright red, heavy display type ("100 PAGES / HARD EXTREME / DOT TO DOT"), a dense dot field with a cartoon hand holding a pen |

**What the interior sample shows**

- **Density:** roughly 500 to 700 dots per page. The numbers visible in the sample run past 600.
- **Subjects:** animal scenes with context, e.g. a monkey (face pre-drawn) and two koalas on a branch with a tree trunk.
- **Hint lines:** faces, eyes, ears, branches and trunks are printed as finished lines, and the dots only fill in the rest. That makes the picture easier to recognise but takes away part of the reveal.
- **Number size:** tiny, about 4 to 5 pt. Many labels touch or overlap their neighbours, and in the busy areas (the koala faces and fur) it is hard to tell which number belongs to which dot.
- **No frame and no page furniture:** no puzzle number, difficulty rating or dot count is printed on the page.
- **Sample banner:** "The number of pages displayed is limited", so I could not check for solutions or front matter.

**Weaknesses you can compete on**

1. **The audience and the print size contradict each other.** The book is sold to seniors but uses about 4 pt numbers in crowded clusters, which is exactly what seniors complain about in reviews.
2. Labels collide, and it is unclear which dot each number belongs to.
3. There is no difficulty ramp and no page metadata.
4. Its single rating means its ranking is weak and the niche is not locked up.

## Book B: *Extreme Dot To Dot Books for Adults: The Ultimate Coloring Book*

| | |
|---|---|
| Author | S.B Lane Austin |
| Format / price | Paperback, **$9.99** |
| Positioning | "+20,000 dots of animals, birds, butterflies, landscapes, flowers and more (mindful dot to dot activity book for adults)" |
| Cover | Lavender band, brush-script title, eagle head drawn in dense dots, a photo of a hand with a pen, and a "+20,000 DOTS" badge |
| Length | About **103+ pages** (the sample says "Pages 7 - 103 are not included") |

**What the interior sample shows**

- **Front matter:** a title page, a QR code that probably links to a bonus or review page, a "This Book Belongs To" line, and a thank-you note recommending colored pencils and alcohol-based markers.
- **Visible difficulty ramp:** page 4 has about 280 dots with bold, readable numbers, while page 6 already has about 690 small dots. The book starts easy and gets dense quickly.
- **Bordered frame** on every puzzle page, which looks tidy and professional.
- **Mostly full-page subjects with no hint lines**, which gives a stronger "reveal" moment than Book A.
- A coloured page with a grid of thumbnails sits after page 103. **This is probably the solutions or answer key.**
- **The "+20,000 dots" claim** works out to about 200 dots per puzzle on average over about 100 puzzles, so the "extreme" label is partly marketing.

**Weaknesses you can compete on**

1. Recommending alcohol markers **bleeds through** ordinary KDP paper. The sample does not show whether pages are single-sided.
2. Difficulty jumps sharply from about 280 to about 690 dots within three pages.
3. The same tiny-number problem appears on the dense pages.

## Market takeaways

1. **The niche is crowded at $9.99 but the quality is low.** Neither title has strong reviews. **Readability** (number size, no clutter) is the biggest differentiator and the easiest to show in "Look inside": put a clean, readable page in the first five pages of the sample.
2. **Use "Extreme" plus a concrete number in the title** ("1,000-dot puzzles", "+60,000 dots"). Both competitors lead with quantity. Our generator reports the exact total dot count, so the claim on the cover is true.
3. **Sell the "large print for seniors" angle as a separate edition** instead of claiming it in a small-print book. That is two listings from one engine.
4. **Subjects that sell:** animals (owls, cats, butterflies, koalas), flowers and mandalas, and seasonal or holiday themes (the Amazon "related" carousel showed *All Seasons Dot to Dot*, Christmas word search and 2027 crossword books). Plan themed spin-offs: Christmas, Halloween, Ocean, Birds, Mandalas.
5. **Cover formula that matches the category:** a bold high-contrast colour band, huge "DOT TO DOT" type, a dense dot picture, a hand holding a pen, and a badge with the dot count.
6. **Quick wins competitors skip:** solutions, a difficulty legend, single-sided pages, a worked example, and a QR code to a free printable bonus page (an email-list builder, as Book B does).
